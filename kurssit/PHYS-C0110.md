## Description

Mechanics is a 5 ECTS introductory university physics course covering classical mechanics at a calculus-based level. It is one of the core physics courses for first- and second-year students in engineering and science programmes. The course covers kinematics and dynamics of particles, Newton's laws, work and energy, momentum and collisions, rotational motion, and oscillations. The treatment is quantitative and relies on calculus; concurrent enrolment in Differential and Integral Calculus 1 is typical. The course builds the physical intuition and problem-solving skills required for all subsequent physics courses at Aalto.

## Contents and workload

### Overall workload

The course runs in Period I and is 5 ECTS compressed into roughly 6–7 weeks. The workload is around 12–15 hours per week. The early weeks on kinematics and Newton's laws are more straightforward, while rotational dynamics and oscillations in the later weeks require significantly more effort. Students who have not studied physics in upper secondary school at a high level often find the initial conceptual jump significant despite the mathematical tools being relatively simple.

### Weekly contents

| Week | Topics |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Measurement and units; kinematics in one dimension; displacement, velocity, acceleration; constant acceleration. |
| 2 | Vectors; kinematics in two and three dimensions; projectile motion; uniform circular motion. |
| 3 | Newton's laws; free body diagrams; friction; applications to inclined planes and pulley systems. |
| 4 | Work and kinetic energy; work–energy theorem; potential energy; conservation of energy. |
| 5 | Linear momentum and collisions; impulse; conservation of momentum; elastic and inelastic collisions; centre of mass. |
| 6 | Rotational kinematics; moment of inertia; torque; rotational dynamics; angular momentum; conservation of angular momentum. |
| 7 | Oscillations; simple harmonic motion; pendulums; energy in SHM; revision and exam preparation. |

## Practicalities

The course is completed via weekly exercises and a final written exam. Exercise sessions are organised in small groups and provide guided problem-solving support. Attending exercise sessions is voluntary (cross check with MyCo) but strongly recommended, especially for the harder weeks. The exam is written, closed-book. The grade is weighted between exercise points and the final exam.
